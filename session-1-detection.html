AI Foundations, Detecting AI to Minimize Real Risk: Is This AI? A Field Guide

You do not need to have attended today's session to use this. Everything you need is here.

What Is AI and How Does it Work?

It's important to have a general understanding of how AI works, what it is, and what it is not. It's also important to note that the rate of AI development is rapid and what may have been true a month ago may now be ancient history.

Why This Is Hard Now

Two years ago, AI-generated text and images had obvious tells. That is no longer reliably true. The technology has improved faster than most people's mental model of it has kept up, which means the confidence you might feel about spotting a fake is often out of date.
This is not a failure of attention on your part. It is the actual current state of the technology, and it is worth knowing that plainly rather than assuming the gap is in you.

What AI Detection Tools Actually Do

Software tools exist that claim to detect AI-generated content — you may have seen names like GPTZero or Originality.ai. Independent testing of these tools in 2026 found accuracy in the range of 80 to 85 percent at best. That means roughly one in six to one in eight judgments these tools make is wrong in one direction or the other. Some human-written text gets flagged as AI. Some AI-written text passes as human. Neither type of error is rare enough to ignore.

It is also worth knowing that this is not a problem that is being solved over time. As AI models improve, they become harder to detect, not easier.
Detection and generation are in a running contest, and generation currently has the advantage. The field is moving toward a different kind of answer: rather than trying to guess where something came from after the fact, the more durable approach is establishing where content came from at the moment it was created — sometimes called provenance. Some regions, including the European Union, are moving toward requiring that AI-generated content be labeled at the source. That is a structural fix rather than a detection contest, and it is likely where things are headed over the next several years.

What You Can Actually Rely On

For text: Be alert to writing that is unnaturally even in tone throughout, that stays generic without offering any specific or checkable detail, and that hedges every claim without ever committing to something concrete. These are tendencies, not proof — a cautious human writer can sound similar, and a well-prompted AI system can avoid all of these tells. Use them as one input among several, not a verdict.

For images: The classic tells — malformed hands, garbled text within an image, lighting that does not quite make physical sense — are still sometimes present, but they are disappearing quickly as the technology improves. Do not build your confidence on these alone, and do not assume their absence means an image is authentic.

For voice: This is the category with the most serious financial consequences, because voice cloning has become good enough that listening carefully is no longer a dependable defense. A cloned voice can now reproduce not just tone but the small verbal habits of a real person. Your ear is not the right tool here.

The One Habit That Actually Protects You

Because none of the detection methods above are reliable enough on their own, the real defense is procedural, not perceptual. If you receive any communication that creates urgency around money or a loved one's safety, follow this every time, without exception for how convincing it seems:

If it involves a family member, agree in advance on a simple verification phrase that only your family would know, and ask for it if anything feels off.

What This Means for You, in Three Sentences

You will not always be able to tell whether something is AI-generated, and that is the honest state of the technology rather than a personal shortcoming. Treat any unexpected urgent request, especially one involving money, as unverified until confirmed through a second channel you control. When in doubt, the habit of verifying matters more than the skill of detecting.

Keep this page. The habit above is the one part of today worth remembering even if everything else fades.
